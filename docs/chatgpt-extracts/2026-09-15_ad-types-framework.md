## 1. Topic Ads

*Ads built around a specific subject/theme (yoga, B2B software, nutrition, etc.)*

### 1.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Topic Ads anchor their messaging to a specific subject that the target audience is already interested in or searching for. The ad's relevance is derived entirely from the theme alignment between ad content and audience intent.

**Why it works:**
- **Relevance trigger:** Humans process personally relevant information 3x faster than generic content
- **Authority signal:** Being "the yoga studio ad" positions the brand as a category expert
- **Cognitive fluency:** Audiences recognize and trust topic-congruent ads, reducing friction

**Best suited audiences:**
- B2C: Consumers actively researching a topic (fitness, cooking, travel)
- B2B: Decision-makers researching a specific solution category (HR software, fleet management)
- Works for both; B2B requires longer form, more proof-points

**When to use:**
- Campaign launch / brand awareness phase
- When target audience has clear, identifiable topic interests
- SEO-complementary SEM campaigns

**When to avoid:**
- Hyper-competitive categories where topic alone won't differentiate
- Audiences unfamiliar with the topic (education required first)

### 1.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Platform | Best Format | Recommended Length | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | Responsive Search Ad (RSA) | 30-char headline × 3, 90-char desc × 2 | "Learn More", "Explore [Topic]" |
| Google Display | Responsive Display Ad | 25-char short headline, 90-char desc | "Discover [Topic]" |
| Meta/Facebook Feed | Single Image or Carousel | Primary text: 125 chars; headline: 40 | "Learn More", "Shop Now" |
| Meta/Facebook Stories | Full-screen vertical video | 15 seconds | Swipe Up / Link |
| Meta/Facebook Reels | Native-style video | 15–30 seconds | "Learn More" overlay |
| Instagram Feed | Single Image or Carousel | Caption: 150 chars recommended | "Learn More", "Explore" |
| LinkedIn Sponsored Content | Single Image + article | Intro: 150 chars; headline: 70 | "Read Article", "Learn More" |
| LinkedIn Message Ads | InMail | 500–1000 words | "Download Guide", "Register" |
| TikTok | TopView / In-Feed Video | 9–15 seconds | "Shop Now", "Learn More" |
| YouTube Pre-roll | TrueView In-Stream | 30–60 seconds (skip at 5s) | End-screen CTA |
| YouTube Mid-roll | Non-skippable | 15–20 seconds | Brand recall focus |
| Twitter/X | Promoted Tweet | 280 chars + image | "Read More", hashtag |
| Pinterest | Standard Pin or Video Pin | 100–300 char description | "Visit Website", "Shop" |

### 1.3 Headline Formulas

1. `"Everything You Need to Know About [TOPIC] — Starting Today"`
2. `"[TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]: The [YEAR] Complete Guide"`
3. `"Your [TOPIC] Journey Starts Here — [BRAND/OFFER]"`
4. `"[NUMBER] [TOPIC] Essentials Every [AUDIENCE] Should Know"`
5. `"The [ADJECTIVE] Way to [BENEFIT] Through [TOPIC]"`

### 1.4 Ad Copy Framework

**Hook:** Open with a bold statement or question that names the topic and audience together. Example: *"If you've been searching for everything yoga can do for your body — you've found the right place."*

**Body (P-A-S):**
- Problem: Most people explore [topic] but never go deep enough to see real results.
- Agitate: You scroll content, try one class, and give up — because no one showed you the full picture.
- Solution: [Brand] gives you a structured path into [topic] with expert instruction, a community, and measurable results.

**CTA:** "Start your [topic] journey today — first class free."

**Full example (yoga):**
> Yoga isn't just stretching. It's a complete system for reducing stress, building strength, and finding clarity — and most people only scratch the surface.
>
> At Soham Yoga, we've guided 2,000+ students from first-timer to consistent practitioner. Our instructors meet you where you are: beginner, athlete, desk-worker, or recovering from injury.
>
> Classes available 7 days a week, in-studio and online. First class is on us.
>
> **Try your first class free →**

### 1.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Clean, aspirational lifestyle photography. Subject engaged in the topic, not posing for the camera.
- **Color psychology:** Align with topic emotion — yoga: teal/sage (calm); tech: navy/white (trust); food: warm amber/red (appetite)
- **Text overlay:** One clear line, max 20% of image area. Brand name + single value phrase.
- **A/B test variables:** Image (solo person vs. group), background color, overlay text on vs. off, headline question vs. statement

### 1.6 Dos ✅

1. Use the topic keyword in the first headline or first line of copy
2. Match visual theme exactly to the topic — no stock photo mismatches
3. Segment by topic sub-category for precision (not "yoga" but "hot yoga for beginners")
4. Link to a landing page dedicated to that exact topic (not the homepage)
5. Use social proof that references the topic specifically ("2,000 yoga students trust us")

### 1.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't mix multiple unrelated topics in one ad — dilutes relevance signal
2. Don't use generic stock imagery that could belong to any brand
3. Don't send topic-specific ad traffic to a generic homepage
4. Don't ignore platform topic-targeting tools (interest targeting, topic keywords)
5. Don't run the same creative for 90+ days without refreshing

### 1.8 Best Practices

1. Layer topic targeting with demographic targeting for precision
2. Create separate ad sets per topic sub-niche
3. Use topic-specific landing pages with matching headline mirroring
4. Rotate 3–5 creative variants to fight ad fatigue
5. Build a topic content series (awareness → education → conversion)

### 1.9 Common Mistakes

1. Too broad a topic ("health" vs. "postpartum yoga recovery")
2. Topic in headline but none in body or landing page
3. No clear conversion path after audience engages with topic content
4. Assuming topic interest = purchase intent (requires funnel nurturing)
5. Forgetting negative audiences (exclude people who already converted)

### 1.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Avoid health claims about topics (yoga "cures" anxiety — not compliant)
- **Platform policy:** Meta restricts "before/after" imagery; health topics require disclaimers
- **Brand risk:** Off-brand topic alignment (sponsoring controversial topics) creates association risk
- **Mitigation:** Legal review of claims; brand safety keyword exclusion lists; platform pre-approval review for health-adjacent topics

## 2. Dos and Don'ts Ads

*Contrast-format ads showing right vs. wrong approach*

### 2.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Dos and Don'ts Ads present a direct contrast between a wrong/suboptimal behavior and the right/better approach. The contrast structure creates immediate cognitive engagement.

**Why it works:**
- **Contrast effect:** The human brain is wired to notice differences; contrast is processed faster than similarity
- **Prescriptive authority:** Telling people what to do/avoid positions brand as expert
- **Validation psychology:** Audiences see their current "mistake" reflected, feel understood, then seek the solution

**Best suited audiences:** B2B professionals (process-heavy decisions), B2C consumers making recurring purchases with clear right/wrong patterns (nutrition, fitness, finance)

**When to use:** Mid-funnel nurturing, retargeting audiences who engaged with awareness content  
**When to avoid:** Early TOFU where audience doesn't yet know enough to recognize the "don't"

### 2.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Platform | Best Format | Recommended Length | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | RSA with contrast in headlines | 30-char headline × 3 | "See How", "Compare Now" |
| Google Display | Split-panel display banner | 300×250 or 728×90 | "See the Difference" |
| Meta/Facebook Feed | Carousel (Don't → Do) or Split-screen video | 60–90 seconds video | "Learn More" |
| Meta Stories | Tap-through contrast panels | 5s per panel | Swipe Up |
| Instagram Feed | Side-by-side image layout | Caption: 150 chars | "Save This" |
| LinkedIn | Document Ad (list format) or Sponsored Content | Intro: 200 chars | "Download" |
| TikTok | Duet-style or split-screen video | 30–45 seconds | "Follow for More" |
| YouTube | Tutorial-style pre-roll | 60 seconds+ | End-screen CTA |
| Twitter/X | Thread format or single tweet | 280 chars + image | "Read Thread" |
| Pinterest | Infographic Pin | Tall 2:3 format | "Visit Website" |

### 2.3 Headline Formulas

1. `"Stop [WRONG BEHAVIOR] — [RIGHT BEHAVIOR] Gets [RESULT] 3x Faster"`
2. `"Don't [COMMON MISTAKE] — Do [BETTER APPROACH] Instead"`
3. `"[AUDIENCE]: Are You [DOING THIS WRONG]? Here's the Right Way"`
4. `"Wrong: [BEHAVIOR A] | Right: [BEHAVIOR B] — Know the Difference"`
5. `"[NUMBER] Things You're Doing Wrong with [TOPIC] (And How to Fix Them)"`

### 2.4 Ad Copy Framework

**Hook:** Call out a specific wrong behavior the audience recognizes in themselves.

**Body:**
- Don't: [Specific wrong behavior] — Here's why it's hurting your [outcome]
- Do: [Specific right behavior] — This is what [successful people/brands] do instead
- Bridge: Making this one switch changed [metric] for [social proof reference]

**CTA:** "See the full dos and don'ts guide →"

**Full example (SaaS project management):**
> **Don't** track projects in a spreadsheet that 6 people edit simultaneously — you'll spend 3 hours a week reconciling version conflicts.
>
> **Do** use a single source of truth where every task has an owner, a deadline, and an auto-updated status visible to the whole team.
>
> Teams that switch from spreadsheet chaos to structured project management report 40% fewer missed deadlines in the first 90 days.
>
> See the full comparison — and try it free for 14 days.
>
> **Compare the difference →**

### 2.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Split-screen or before/after layout; left side shows "Don't" (muted/red tones), right side shows "Do" (bright/green tones)
- **Color psychology:** Red = wrong, danger; Green = right, success. Never invert this.
- **Text overlay:** Bold "DON'T ❌" / "DO ✅" labels — clear hierarchy
- **A/B test variables:** Static split-screen vs. animated reveal; red/green vs. gray/color; text-heavy vs. image-heavy

### 2.6 Dos ✅

1. Make the "don't" behavior specific and relatable — not generic
2. Ensure the "do" behavior is achievable, not aspirational fantasy
3. Use the brand's product/service as the natural enabler of the "do"
4. Keep contrast visual — don't make the audience read to understand the contrast
5. Use real data or customer testimonials to validate the "do" behavior

### 2.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't shame the audience — the "don't" should be sympathetic, not judgmental
2. Don't make the "don't" an implicit attack on a competitor (brand safety risk)
3. Don't present more than 3 contrasts in one ad (cognitive overload)
4. Don't use ambiguous contrasts that require explanation to understand
5. Don't forget to show the outcome — contrast without result = incomplete argument

### 2.8 Best Practices

1. Test "Don't/Do" frame vs. "Wrong/Right" frame — tone varies by audience
2. Use the contrast as a retargeting trigger for high-intent audiences
3. Follow up the ad with a content piece (blog, video) that expands the list
4. Track "save" rate on Instagram — high saves = high resonance
5. Sequence: Dos/Don'ts awareness → product demo → free trial offer

### 2.9 Common Mistakes

1. Making the "do" so obvious it's patronizing
2. Contrast that's too abstract ("Don't be inefficient → Do be efficient")
3. No brand/product connection to the "do" outcome
4. Platform mismatch — detailed contrast works in LinkedIn, not TikTok
5. Failing to address objections (why the wrong behavior feels easier)

### 2.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Comparative claims ("X is wrong, our way is right") must not defame competitors
- **Platform policy:** Meta restricts "before/after" for weight loss, health, appearance
- **Brand risk:** If the "don't" is something the brand previously promoted, creates credibility gap
- **Mitigation:** Frame "don'ts" as industry habits, not competitor products; legal pre-clearance for health/finance verticals

## 3. Best Practices Ads

*Authority-positioning ads that share proven methods*

### 3.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Best Practices Ads position the brand as the authoritative source of proven, expert-validated methodology. They share actionable standards in the ad itself — building trust before asking for anything.

**Why it works:**
- **Authority bias:** Humans defer to perceived experts, especially in complex domains
- **Reciprocity:** Giving genuine value in the ad creates obligation to reciprocate (click, follow, purchase)
- **Proof framing:** "Best practice" language implies field validation beyond personal opinion

**Best suited audiences:** B2B buyers evaluating vendors, professionals seeking career/skill improvement, B2C consumers making considered purchases  
**When to use:** Thought leadership phases, content marketing amplification  
**When to avoid:** Pure impulse-purchase categories; audiences seeking entertainment, not instruction

### 3.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Platform | Best Format | Recommended Length | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | RSA with authority signals | "Proven Method", "Industry Standard" | "Learn Best Practices" |
| Google Display | Educational banner with list preview | 300×250 | "Download Guide" |
| Meta/Facebook Feed | Carousel (one practice per slide) | 5–7 slides, 50 chars/slide | "Get the Full List" |
| Meta Stories | Quick-tip series | 5–7 second panels | "Swipe for Next Tip" |
| Instagram Feed | Infographic carousel | 5–10 slides | "Save This" |
| LinkedIn | Document Ad / Article | Long-form 800–1500 words | "Follow", "Download" |
| TikTok | Educational video ("Did you know?") | 30–60 seconds | "Follow for More Tips" |
| YouTube | Tutorial/explainer pre-roll | 60–120 seconds | "Subscribe", "Learn More" |
| Twitter/X | Thread of best practices | 5–10 tweets | "Retweet to Save" |
| Pinterest | Checklist or step-by-step Pin | Tall 2:3 | "Visit Website" |

### 3.3 Headline Formulas

1. `"[NUMBER] [TOPIC] Best Practices Every [AUDIENCE] Should Follow"`
2. `"The [INDUSTRY]-Proven Method for [OUTCOME] — Used by [SOCIAL PROOF]"`
3. `"Industry Standard: How [AUDIENCE] Achieve [RESULT] in [TIMEFRAME]"`
4. `"[YEAR] Best Practices for [TOPIC]: Updated by [CREDENTIAL]"`
5. `"What High-Performing [AUDIENCE] Do Differently — Best Practice Guide"`

### 3.4 Ad Copy Framework

**Hook:** Open with a credential or outcome statement that signals authority. *"After working with 500+ yoga students, here's the single most important practice we recommend."*

**Body:** Present 2–3 specific, actionable best practices. Each one should be concrete enough that the reader gains value just from reading it.

**CTA:** "Get the complete best practices guide — [NUMBER] more practices inside."

**Full example (B2B SaaS — project management):**
> After 10 years working with engineering teams, here are the 3 project management best practices that separate high-performing teams from the rest:
>
> **1. Every task has one owner, not a team.** Shared ownership = no ownership.
> **2. Status updates happen in the tool, not in meetings.** If it's not logged, it didn't happen.
> **3. Retrospectives run on data, not memory.** Review metrics every sprint, not feelings.
>
> These practices reduced missed deadlines by 42% for our customers in 2025.
>
> **Get the full 15-practice guide — free download →**

### 3.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Clean, structured. Numbered lists, checkmarks, credentials/logos visible
- **Color psychology:** Navy/white = authority; forest green = expertise; dark gray = professionalism
- **Text overlay:** The best practice title/number should be readable without clicking
- **A/B test variables:** Number of practices shown (3 vs. 5), credential reference (customer count vs. years), image vs. clean text background

### 3.6 Dos ✅

1. Back each best practice with data, customer results, or research citation
2. Make practices specific and actionable, not vague ("Always A/B test" → "A/B test one variable at a time, run for 14 days minimum")
3. Reference credibility: years of experience, customer count, industry recognition
4. Link to a full content piece — the ad is the preview, not the full value
5. Tailor practices to the specific audience segment (beginner vs. advanced)

### 3.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't list generic advice that any blog could have written
2. Don't claim "best practice" status without supporting evidence
3. Don't write practices so basic they feel obvious to the target audience
4. Don't bury the brand — authority comes from the brand, so it should be visible
5. Don't forget to include a content upgrade (guide, checklist) as the CTA offer

### 3.8 Best Practices

1. Gate the full best practices list behind an email opt-in for lead gen
2. Update "best practices" content seasonally to maintain relevance
3. Source practices from real customer data and present the methodology
4. Repurpose into multiple formats: ad, blog, email series, webinar
5. Use LinkedIn Document Ads for B2B — highest engagement for list content

### 3.9 Common Mistakes

1. Practices that are too theoretical with no implementation guidance
2. No differentiation — practices identical to what 10 competitors already publish
3. Over-claiming ("the only best practice framework you'll ever need")
4. No measurement criteria for whether a practice is working
5. Single creative execution across all platforms — format must match platform norms

### 3.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** "Industry standard" and "proven" require substantiation
- **Platform policy:** Health/financial best practices require disclaimers
- **Brand risk:** If practices later prove incorrect, brand authority suffers
- **Mitigation:** Add "based on our customer data" qualifiers; annual content review process; include disclaimer for regulated industries

## 4. Knowledge Ads

*Educational/informational ads that teach something valuable*

### 4.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Knowledge Ads deliver a genuine learning moment inside the ad itself — a fact, framework, insight, or explanation the audience didn't know before. The value exchange happens before any click is required.

**Why it works:**
- **Curiosity gap:** Opening with an intriguing fact creates an itch to know more
- **Novelty bias:** New information triggers dopamine — the brain actively seeks novel knowledge
- **Halo effect:** Brands that teach you something are perceived as more trustworthy and expert

**Best suited audiences:** Intellectually curious B2B buyers, educated B2C consumers, audiences in research/consideration phase  
**When to use:** TOFU awareness building, content marketing amplification, newsletter audience building  
**When to avoid:** Pure BOFU conversion campaigns; price-driven audiences who aren't seeking information

- **Text overlay:** The surprising fact or number should be the largest visual element
- **A/B test variables:** Surprising stat vs. practical tip, infographic vs. video explainer, brand-forward vs. content-forward

### 4.6 Dos ✅

1. Verify every fact, statistic, and research reference before publishing
2. Make the knowledge immediately useful — "so what?" must be answered in the ad
3. Match complexity level to audience — don't over-simplify for experts or over-explain for beginners
4. Follow up knowledge with a content piece that deepens the learning
5. Source your data visibly — "Based on [Study/Organization]" builds credibility

### 4.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't use fabricated statistics or unverified claims
2. Don't make the knowledge so complex it requires prior expertise to understand
3. Don't bury the learning insight behind brand messaging — knowledge first, brand second
4. Don't forget attribution — "studies show" without specifics is weak
5. Don't make every knowledge ad a sales pitch — let the learning stand on its own

### 4.8 Best Practices

1. Build a content calendar of knowledge ads around seasonal relevance
2. Use knowledge ads as top-of-funnel to build retargeting audiences
3. Link to expanded content (research summary, explainer video) — not a sales page
4. A/B test the surprising element: statistic vs. mechanism vs. myth-bust
5. Repurpose high-performing knowledge ads into email newsletter content

### 4.9 Common Mistakes

1. "Knowledge" that's actually just product marketing dressed up as insight
2. Sourcing issues — citing unverifiable or overstated research
3. No practical application — reader learns but can't act on the knowledge
4. Audience mismatch — experts don't want basics; beginners need context first
5. One-and-done approach — knowledge ads build best over a content series

### 4.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Health/medical knowledge claims require disclaimers; financial knowledge requires regulatory compliance
- **Platform policy:** Medical claims, drug references, financial advice all trigger policy flags

## 5. Mistake-Based Ads

*Ads that open with a common error the audience makes*

### 5.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Mistake-Based Ads begin by identifying a specific, recognizable error the target audience is making — then position the brand as the solution. The "uh oh, that's me" moment creates instant engagement.

**Why it works:**
- **Pain recognition:** Seeing your own mistake reflected back triggers immediate attention
- **Shame/relief cycle:** Brief discomfort at recognition → relief when solution is offered
- **Confirmation bias flip:** Audience had a belief (their approach was fine) — the ad disrupts it productively

**Best suited audiences:** Mid-funnel audiences who are aware of the problem category but haven't found the solution; people actively trying an approach that isn't working  
**When to use:** MOFU retargeting, competitive conquesting, product launch disruption  
**When to avoid:** TOFU awareness (audience doesn't know enough to recognize the mistake)

### 5.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Platform | Best Format | Recommended Length | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | RSA with mistake-opening headline | "Are You [Mistake]?" | "Fix This Now", "See Solution" |

| Meta/Facebook Feed | Short video (problem reveal) | 30–60 seconds | "Stop Doing This" |
| Meta Stories | Quick mistake callout | 7–10 seconds | Swipe for solution |
| Instagram Feed | "Stop [Doing X]" hook image | Bold text on image | "Save This" |
| LinkedIn | Thought leadership post | 200–300 char intro | "See the Fix" |
| TikTok | "POV: You're doing [mistake]" | 15–30 seconds | Stitch/Duet response |
| YouTube | Pre-roll with mistake hook first 5 seconds | 30–60 seconds | "Fix This" end-screen |
| Twitter/X | Thread opening with bold mistake claim | 280 chars + thread | Reply/RT |
| Pinterest | "You're doing [X] wrong" Pin | Checklist format | Visit for fix |

### 5.3 Headline Formulas

1. `"Stop [DOING X] — [BETTER ALTERNATIVE] Works 3x Better"`
2. `"This [COMMON MISTAKE] Is Costing [AUDIENCE] [QUANTIFIED LOSS]"`
3. `"[NUMBER]% of [AUDIENCE] Make This [TOPIC] Mistake — Are You One?"`
4. `"If You're [DOING THIS], You're [LEAVING RESULT ON THE TABLE]"`
5. `"The [TOPIC] Mistake That Took Me [TIMEFRAME] to Unlearn"`

### 5.4 Ad Copy Framework

**Hook (first 3 seconds/first line):** Name the specific mistake with enough precision that it stings a little. Not "bad project management" but "You're reviewing project status in a weekly meeting instead of a live dashboard."

**Body:**
- Establish why this mistake is costly (time, money, relationships, health)
- Show that the mistake is common — normalize it, reduce shame
- Present the better path with a specific, credible improvement claim

**CTA:** "Stop [mistake] — here's the [N]-step fix →"

**Full example (fitness/yoga):**
> **If you're stretching before your yoga class — stop.**
>
> Cold static stretching before movement reduces muscular power by up to 8% and increases injury risk. Most people learned this in gym class and never unlearned it.
>
> The correct warm-up is dynamic movement: Sun Salutations, gentle spinal rotations, hip openers. Save the deep stretching for after class.

>
> **Book a class and see the difference in how you feel →**

### 5.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Relatable "mistake in progress" imagery — someone doing the wrong thing, captured mid-action. Not dramatic, just recognizable.
- **Color psychology:** Warm amber/orange for the "mistake" state → shift to calming blue/green for solution
- **Text overlay:** The mistake should be immediately visible in bold — "❌ Stop doing this"
- **A/B test variables:** First-person mistake ("I used to do this") vs. second-person ("You're making this mistake"), mistake image vs. solution image as thumbnail

### 5.6 Dos ✅

1. Make the mistake extremely specific — the more precise, the more recognition
2. Normalize the mistake — "most people do this" reduces shame and increases relatability
3. Show the mistake costs something real (time, money, health, results)
4. Offer the fix within the ad itself — don't just identify the problem
5. Sequence: mistake ad → solution content → product as the complete solution

### 5.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't shame the audience — empathy, not judgment
2. Don't invent a mistake that doesn't actually exist in the audience's behavior
3. Don't open with the solution — the mistake must land first for the framework to work
4. Don't be preachy or repetitive about the mistake
5. Don't make the "fix" so complex it creates new overwhelm

### 5.8 Best Practices

1. Research real customer support data and sales objections for genuine mistake material
2. Test which mistake resonates most across audience segments (multivariate)
3. Use mistake-based ads as retargeting triggers for video viewers
4. Pair with a "how I fixed it" customer story for social proof
5. Run A/B: mistake-first frame vs. solution-first frame — audience preference varies

### 5.9 Common Mistakes

1. Mistake so obvious it feels insulting to the audience
2. No connection between the mistake and the brand's specific solution
3. Too many mistakes in one ad — pick the single most resonant one
4. Using a mistake that implies the competitor's product is the "wrong" choice (comparative claim risk)
5. Mistake hook in the ad, but landing page opens with benefits — message mismatch

### 5.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Quantified improvement claims ("3x better") require substantiation
- **Platform policy:** "Before/after" imagery for health/body mistakes restricted on Meta
- **Brand risk:** If the "mistake" is something the brand itself taught previously, creates credibility gap
- **Mitigation:** Pair claims with "up to" qualifiers and source citations; legal review for health claims

## 6. Risk-Based Ads

*Ads that highlight danger, loss, or downside of inaction*

### 6.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Risk-Based Ads activate loss aversion — one of the most powerful psychological forces in decision-making. They make the cost of NOT acting visceral and immediate.

**Why it works:**
- **Loss aversion:** Kahneman's research shows losses feel 2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good
- **Status quo bias disruption:** People need a jolt to break from inaction; risk framing provides it
- **FOMO amplification:** Not just fear of missing out — fear of real, quantified loss

**Best suited audiences:** Audiences who know they have a problem but haven't acted; high-stakes purchase decisions (insurance, legal, financial, health); risk-averse B2B buyers  
**When to use:** MOFU/BOFU conversion campaigns; time-sensitive offers; compliance-driven purchases  
**When to avoid:** Audiences with anxiety disorders (health/pharma); audiences in vulnerable states; excessive use causes ad fatigue and brand negative association

### 6.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Meta Stories | Urgent one-panel | 5–7 seconds | Tap for solution |
| Instagram Feed | Risk stat image | Bold number/statistic | "See Solution" |
| LinkedIn | Sponsored Content with data | 150 char intro with statistic | "Assess Your Risk" |
| TikTok | "What happens if you don't [X]" | 30 seconds | "Save This Warning" |
| YouTube | Problem consequence pre-roll | 30–60 seconds | "Act Before It's Too Late" |
| Twitter/X | Risk data tweet | Thread with escalating stakes | "Don't Be a Statistic" |
| Pinterest | Risk infographic | Warning-style layout | "Protect Yourself" |

2. `"The Hidden Cost of Ignoring [PROBLEM] — [SCARY STAT]"`

4. `"[TIMEFRAME] Without [SOLUTION] = [SPECIFIC CONSEQUENCE]"`
5. `"By the Time [AUDIENCE] Realize [RISK], [CONSEQUENCE IS ALREADY HAPPENING]"`

### 6.4 Ad Copy Framework

**Hook:** Open with a stark, specific risk statistic or scenario. Make it concrete, not theoretical.

**Body:**
- Quantify the risk: real numbers, real consequences
- Personalize the risk: "If you're [audience profile], here's your specific exposure"
- Provide an escape: the brand's solution as the risk-eliminator

**CTA:** "Protect yourself now — [specific action]"

>
> Our retirement planning tool shows your personal gap in 3 minutes. Free, no obligation.
>
> **See your real retirement gap →**

### 6.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Visualization of consequence — not fear-porn imagery, but data visualization (gap charts, countdown timers, cost calculators)
- **Color psychology:** Deep red/amber = urgency/warning; then transition to calm blue/green for solution
- **Text overlay:** The risk number or statistic should dominate the visual
- **A/B test variables:** Real statistic vs. personal scenario, calm data presentation vs. urgent warning tone, risk-first vs. solution-first

### 6.6 Dos ✅

1. Make the risk quantifiable — vague risk doesn't drive action
2. Personalize the risk to the specific audience segment
3. Transition quickly from risk to solution — don't dwell on fear
4. Use credible sources for all risk statistics
5. Include a clear, immediate next step to mitigate the risk

### 6.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't create false or exaggerated risks — legal and ethical violation
2. Don't use manipulative imagery (children in danger, explicit suffering)
3. Don't leave the audience in fear without providing the solution
4. Don't overuse risk framing — audiences disengage from constant alarm
5. Don't apply risk framing to trivial decisions (over-proportion)

### 6.8 Best Practices

1. A/B test the specificity of risk: general percentage vs. personalized dollar amount
2. Follow risk ads with a solution ad in the retargeting sequence
3. Use countdown urgency sparingly — only when the deadline is real
4. Pair with a free risk assessment tool to capture leads
5. Monitor frequency carefully — risk fatigue sets in faster than other ad types

### 6.9 Common Mistakes

1. Risk so extreme it triggers disbelief rather than action
2. No actionable step provided — risk without escape = helplessness, not motivation
3. Targeting audiences who are already anxious (ethical and performance problem)
4. Risk that doesn't apply to the target segment — loses credibility immediately
5. Risk framing without brand trust signals — source credibility matters for risk claims

### 6.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Risk claims must be accurate; financial risk projections need regulatory disclaimers
- **Platform policy:** Fear-based health claims restricted on Meta and Google; cannot imply diagnoses
- **Brand risk:** Constant risk messaging can make a brand feel threatening, not helpful
- **Mitigation:** Always pair risk with solution and path forward; balance with value-positive ads; include disclaimers for regulated industries

## 7. Value-Focused Ads

*Ads centered on ROI, outcome, transformation*

### 7.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Value-Focused Ads lead with the end outcome — the transformation, result, or return the customer will experience. They answer "what's in it for me?" before any question is asked.

**Why it works:**
- **Outcome orientation:** Customers buy results, not products; value ads speak the customer's actual language
- **Aspirational pull:** The promised outcome activates desire and future-self identity
- **WIIFM (What's In It For Me):** The most fundamental question in all persuasion, answered upfront

**Best suited audiences:** All segments — value framing works universally when outcome is specific and credible  
**When to use:** Full funnel — awareness (aspirational outcome), conversion (specific ROI claim)  
**When to avoid:** Commoditized categories where value claims are interchangeable; without differentiated evidence

### 7.2 Ad Formats Per Platform

| Platform | Best Format | Recommended Length | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | RSA with outcome headline | Transformation in 30 chars | "Get Started", "See Results" |
| Google Display | Testimonial/result banner | Before-state → after-state | "See How" |
| Meta/Facebook Feed | Video testimonial or result showcase | 60–90 seconds | "Get Results Like This" |

## 9. Cost-Saving Focused Ads

*Ads about reducing expenses, eliminating waste, better pricing*

### 9.1 Definition & Core Psychology

**What it is:** Cost-Saving Focused Ads lead with the financial benefit — how much money the customer will save, waste eliminated, or better pricing compared to current situation.

**Why it works:**
- **Loss aversion (financial):** Overpaying feels like losing money — loss aversion again, specific to finance
- **Anchoring:** Presenting the current cost next to the new cost makes savings visceral
- **Economic anxiety:** In inflationary environments, cost-saving framing outperforms most other frames

**Best suited audiences:** Price-sensitive B2C consumers; CFO/procurement-focused B2B buyers; SMB owners managing tight margins  
**When to use:** Economic downturns; price-competitive categories; switching campaigns; BOFU where cost is a final objection  
**When to avoid:** Premium/luxury positioning where low-price association damages brand

2. Don't use cost-saving messaging for premium products — repositions brand as budget
3. Don't ignore switching costs — "just save money" without addressing the migration
4. Don't present savings that only apply to optimal usage scenarios
5. Don't forget non-financial costs — time spent on cheaper alternatives can cost more

### 9.8 Best Practices

1. Build a "cost of doing nothing" calculator (opportunity cost framing)
2. Use competitor conquesting campaigns with cost comparison messaging
3. Target audiences with budget constraints explicitly (job titles: CFO, VP Finance, Owner)
4. Monthly vs. annual pricing anchoring: show annual savings, charge monthly
5. Pair cost-saving with quality assurance — "same quality, lower cost" is more credible than "cheaper"

### 9.9 Common Mistakes

1. Savings claims based on "up to" maximums rather than average case
2. Comparing to a deliberately overpriced competitor to make savings look larger
3. Forgetting that cost-saving ads attract price-sensitive customers who churn faster
4. No quality signal alongside the cost claim — purely cheap = commodity positioning
5. Competing only on price without any differentiation (race to the bottom)

### 9.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** Comparative pricing requires current, accurate competitor data; FTC requirements for savings claims
- **Platform policy:** Google Ads restricts comparative advertising; LinkedIn has brand safety standards
- **Brand risk:** Cost-saving positioning permanently anchors brand as budget — hard to move upmarket
- **Mitigation:** Legal review of all comparative claims; use "vs. industry average" instead of named competitors; qualify all savings with realistic assumptions

### 10.5 Visual / Creative Direction

- **Image style:** Screen recordings, product UI, technology process diagrams, real output examples
- **Color psychology:** Deep blue/indigo = technology/trust; electric cyan = innovation/AI; dark backgrounds = tech/modern
- **Text overlay:** Speed metric ("47 seconds") or capability statement prominently displayed
- **A/B test variables:** Screen recording demo vs. animated explainer, AI output example vs. product UI, feature-led vs. outcome-led

### 10.6 Dos ✅

1. Show the technology working — demos outperform descriptions every time
2. Quantify the capability improvement (speed, accuracy, scale)
3. Address the "is this real?" skepticism with transparent methodology
4. Demonstrate local/private AI if data privacy is a concern for the audience
5. Connect the technology to a specific workflow pain the audience recognizes

### 10.7 Don'ts ❌

1. Don't use "AI-powered" without explaining what the AI actually does
2. Don't show idealized demos that don't reflect real product experience
3. Don't target non-technical audiences with technical jargon
4. Don't promise technology capabilities that are not yet production-ready
5. Don't ignore adoption friction — technical products require onboarding support

### 10.8 Best Practices

1. Use real product screen recordings — not polished motion graphics of fake UIs
2. Include a proof-of-work: real output generated by the tool in the ad
3. Target technical decision-makers separately from end-users — different messaging
4. Build a technology comparison page linked from the ad (vs. manual process vs. competitors)
5. Offer a sandbox/demo environment — technology buyers want to test before buying

### 10.9 Common Mistakes

1. Feature list dump without any context of why features matter
2. Technical language that alienates non-technical decision-makers (often the buyer)
3. Demo video that shows a pristine version of the product, not the real experience
4. No ROI connection — technology for its own sake doesn't drive purchases
5. Ignoring the change management cost — "switch to our tech" requires more than clicking "buy"

### 10.10 Risk Considerations

- **Legal:** AI capability claims must be substantiated; "AI" used as a marketing term without real AI constitutes deceptive advertising
- **Platform policy:** Financial AI tools, medical AI, legal AI tools face stricter scrutiny
- **Brand risk:** Technology that fails in customer hands after bold ad claims causes severe reputation damage
- **Mitigation:** Accurate demo videos; beta testing before launch campaigns; legal review for AI claims in regulated industries; clear limitations disclosure

## Campaign Sequencing Strategy

### Funnel Architecture

**TOFU — Top of Funnel: Awareness & Interest (Day 1–7)**
- **Topic Ads:** Establish brand in the target topic category
- **Knowledge Ads:** Deliver a learning moment — earn attention through value
- **Best Practices Ads:** Build authority positioning through expert content

*Goal: Reach 1,000+ unique prospects per $500 spend. Build retargeting audiences.*

**MOFU — Middle of Funnel: Consideration & Evaluation (Day 8–21)**
- **Mistake-Based Ads:** Disrupt the status quo — show current approach has a better alternative
- **Risk-Based Ads:** Activate loss aversion — cost of inaction becomes real
- **Dos & Don'ts Ads:** Create contrast — differentiate brand's approach from inferior alternatives

*Goal: Convert 10–20% of TOFU engaged audience to MOFU consideration. Capture leads.*

**BOFU — Bottom of Funnel: Decision & Conversion (Day 22–30)**
- **Value-Focused Ads:** Close with specific outcomes, testimonials, and ROI proof
- **Cost-Saving Focused Ads:** Address the price objection — make the math undeniable
- **Productivity-Focused Ads:** Show the immediate, tangible day-1 benefit

*Goal: Convert 5–15% of MOFU leads to paying customers. Maximize ROAS.*

**Retention & Expansion (Day 30+)**
- **Technology-Focused Ads:** Show new features to existing customers — expand usage
- **Best Practices Ads:** Educate customers on using the product better — reduce churn
- **Value-Focused Ads:** Reinforce the decision — testimonials, community, results sharing

*Goal: Reduce churn by 20–30%. Increase LTV through upsell.*

## Ad Compliance & Governance Checklist

### Pre-Launch Checklist by Ad Type

#### FTC Disclosure Requirements (All Ad Types)

- [ ] All testimonials represent typical results OR include "results may vary" disclosure
- [ ] Income claims are substantiated with real customer data
- [ ] "Sponsored", "Ad", or "#ad" clearly labeled on social platforms
- [ ] Health claims reviewed by legal/medical counsel for substantiation
- [ ] No deceptive pricing (original price must be genuine, not inflated for discount)

#### Platform-Specific Policies

**Meta/Facebook/Instagram:**
- [ ] No "before/after" imagery for health, weight, appearance changes (prohibited)
- [ ] No claims of medical outcomes or health conditions (restricted)
- [ ] Financial products require special advertiser certification
- [ ] No misleading subscription terms in copy

**Google Ads:**
- [ ] No comparative claims without verification of competitor data accuracy
- [ ] Healthcare and pharmaceutical ads require certification
- [ ] Financial services require Google certification
- [ ] Payday loan / high-interest products face restrictions

**LinkedIn:**
- [ ] Employment/recruitment ads require accurate job descriptions
- [ ] Financial service ads follow LinkedIn financial advertising policy
- [ ] No claims that make professional comparisons to individuals

#### Industry-Specific Regulatory Compliance

**Healthcare Ads (Risk-Based, Value-Focused, Technology):**
- [ ] No guaranteed outcomes — "may help", "shown to support" language required
- [ ] Medical device claims reviewed against FDA/Health Canada requirements
- [ ] Testimonials from healthcare professionals require credentials verification
- [ ] HIPAA compliance for any audience data used in targeting

**Financial/Insurance Ads (Cost-Saving, Value-Focused, Risk-Based):**
- [ ] Disclosure: "Past performance is not indicative of future results" (investments)
- [ ] Insurance ads comply with provincial/state insurance advertising regulations
- [ ] Licensed professional required for financial advice claims
- [ ] Return/yield projections include assumption disclosures

**Legal Services Ads (Dos/Don'ts, Best Practices):**
- [ ] "Not legal advice" disclaimer required
- [ ] Cannot guarantee legal outcomes
- [ ] State/provincial bar advertising rules apply
- [ ] Attorney testimonials require specific disclosures

#### Brand Safety Guidelines (All Types)

- [ ] Creative reviewed against brand voice guidelines
- [ ] No associations with controversial keywords, topics, or accounts via placement targeting
- [ ] Competitor mentions reviewed by legal for comparative advertising compliance
- [ ] Cultural sensitivity review for international campaigns
- [ ] Tone consistency — Risk-Based ads don't create brand anxiety association if overused

#### Ad Type-Specific Compliance Notes

| Ad Type | Key Compliance Risk | Required Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Topic Ads | Health/wellness topic claims | "Educational purposes" disclaimer; consult professional |
| Dos/Don'ts | Shaming language, comparative claims | Empathy review; no competitor implicit attacks |
| Best Practices | "Industry standard" claims | Source substantiation; "based on our data" qualifiers |
| Knowledge Ads | Factual accuracy, citation quality | Fact-check against primary sources; include citation |
| Mistake-Based | Shame/blame tone, false premises | Empathy review; validate mistake is real |
| Risk-Based | Fear manipulation, false statistics | Evidence requirement; solution required in every ad |
| Value-Focused | FTC testimonial rules, outcome claims | "Individual results may vary"; documented customer data |
| Productivity-Focused | Time-saving quantification accuracy | Based on customer averages, not optimal scenarios |
| Cost-Saving | Comparative pricing accuracy | Current competitor pricing verification; legal review |
| Technology-Focused | AI capability claims, data privacy | Technical accuracy review; privacy disclosures for AI |
